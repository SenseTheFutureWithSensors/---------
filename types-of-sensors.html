Sensors: Types of Sensors

Sensors are the sensory organs of modern technology. They convert physical phenomena—such as heat, light, sound, acceleration, pressure, and gas molecules—into measurable electrical signals. 01 — Taxonomy & Fundamentals

How Sensors are
Classified.

Sensors are categorized according to how they produce signals, whether they require external power, whether they touch the subject, and the physical domains they measure.

By Output Signal

Continuous vs Discrete

The nature of the electrical signal produced determines how a microcontroller interfaces with the sensor.

  • Analog Sensors: Output a continuous voltage or current directly proportional to the physical quantity (e.g., 0V to 5V). Must be converted to numbers by an Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC). Examples: LDR, Thermocouple, LM35, Potentiometer.
  • Digital Sensors: Convert measured data internally into discrete binary numbers or packetized bus data (I2C, SPI, 1-Wire, UART). Highly immune to line noise and cable losses. Examples: DHT22, BME280, MPU6050, DS18B20.

By Energy / Power Source

Self-Generated vs Excited

Determines whether the transducer requires auxiliary electrical energy to modulate signals.

  • Active Sensors: Require an external power supply to energize the sensing circuit or emit probe signals. Examples: Ultrasonic sensor (emits sound), PIR motion detector, Strain Gauge (Wheatstone bridge excitation), Hall switch.
  • Passive Sensors: Generate an output voltage or current autonomously by harvesting energy directly from the physical stimulus itself. Examples: Thermocouples (Seebeck effect), Piezoelectric crystals, Photovoltaic cells.

By Contact Mode

Touch vs Remote

Whether physical touch is necessary to gather accurate readings from the target medium.

  • Contact Sensors: Must make physical surface contact with the medium or object (e.g., RTD temperature probes, limit switches, strain gauges, soil moisture probes).
  • Non-Contact Sensors: Measure attributes across free space via radiation, sound, or magnetic flux (e.g., Infrared thermometers, ToF Laser distance, Ultrasonic, Hall effect).

By Measurand & Domain

Physical Properties

Classification based on the type of physical property or environmental variable being monitored.

  • Thermal: Temperature, heat flux, thermal conductivity.
  • Mechanical: Force, pressure, acceleration, torque, mass, strain.
  • Optical & Radiation: Lux, photon wavelength, UV index, IR radiation.
  • Chemical & Biological: Gas concentration, pH, humidity, SpO2, glucose.

03 — Operating Principle

The Sensor Signal Chain.

Every sensor follows a structured physical-to-digital transformation path before raw reality becomes actionable software data.

STEP 01

Physical Stimulus

Real-world energy: photons, thermal motion, acoustic wave, gas molecules, or gravitational acceleration.

STEP 02

Transducer Element

Converts physical property into electrical change (e.g. resistance change, capacitance shift, or microvolts).

STEP 03

Signal Conditioning

Operational amplifiers boost micro-signals; low-pass filters eliminate electrical noise and frequency aliasing.

STEP 04

Quantization & ADC

Continuous electrical curves are sampled and quantized into discrete digital binary words (10-bit to 24-bit).

STEP 05

MCU & Action

Microcontroller (ESP32/Arduino) applies calibrations, logs data to cloud IoT dashboards, or triggers actuators.
